# Data and evaluation for an adaptive full-duplex speech model

Research date: **2026-11-14**. Scope: a solo developer, Apple M5 MacBook Pro with 32 GB, almost no budget, and an existing cascaded “same words, different voice” evaluation harness.

## How to read this report

**Confirmed** means the linked primary paper, author repository, dataset card, or distributor page was opened and supported the statement. It does not mean that I downloaded the corpus, audited consent, ran the model, or reproduced a result. **Not found / unverified** means exactly that; it is not evidence of absence. Recommendations and engineering estimates are labelled separately. Dataset licences, code licences, model-weight licences, and rights in generated audio are different things. An arXiv paper's licence is not the dataset licence.

Hours below retain the source's convention. Conversation-clock hours, summed speaker-hours, active speech hours, and hours including silence must not be treated as interchangeable. A stereo container also does not prove speaker isolation: both speakers can be audible in both microphones.

**Register/slang answer:** I found **no verified open speech-to-speech dataset specifically providing matched formal/casual/slang user inputs with target assistant register accommodation**, particularly with isolated simultaneous streams. This is a scoped search finding, not a universal nonexistence claim. Related accommodation research exists: LDC's **English Speed Networking Conversational Transcripts** studies effects of power/personality on linguistic style, but it distributes **transcripts**, not the required paired speech targets. [Primary catalog](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2016T16).

“Professional” versus “casual” can involve both lexical register and prosody. If slang words change, that is **not a same-words acoustic intervention**. Evaluate lexical accommodation and acoustic delivery separately. A serious voice does not reliably identify a profession, and distress should elicit support rather than imitation of distress. These are proposed task definitions, not properties guaranteed by the datasets.

**Practical implication:** independently render user and assistant turns, then place them on a common clock. Whole-dialogue TTS can be a listening reference, but source-separating its finished waveform introduces avoidable uncertainty about who spoke when. The proposed per-event method follows the measured-timestamp approach of the 2026 harness above.

Rendering data locally is much more clearly feasible than reproducing the official CUDA training stack on a 32 GB Mac; do not spend weeks making data before confirming the intended trainer can load one batch.

### The benchmark-gap answer

**A broad “none exists” answer is wrong.** StyleTalk and EchoMind explicitly change delivery while holding words fixed. EMO-Reasoning evaluates full-duplex models' emotional responses. InteractionBench even tests whether full-duplex systems lower their voices in response to whispering. [StyleTalk](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12786v2), [EchoMind](https://hlt-cuhksz.github.io/EchoMind/), [EMO-Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17623v1), [InteractionBench](https://avera-labs.github.io/InteractionBench/).

**The narrower gap remains:** I did not verify a released, ready-to-run benchmark jointly providing **paired identical-word delivery interventions, live/streaming Moshi execution, register/slang accommodation, supportive distress responses, output pacing/tone, and overlap/backchannel regression**. Reuse existing stimuli and scorers where licensed, and add the missing streaming and paired-response protocol. FDB v1.5's post-interruption prosody metrics alone do not establish emotion- or register-conditioned accommodation.

## 1. Recommended recipe: roughly 20–50 hours near zero cost

This is a **proposed experiment**, not a recipe demonstrated end-to-end on this M5. Target **conversation-clock duration** with separate reports of active user/assistant hours, silence and overlap. Do not get “50 hours” by adding the duration of two simultaneous channels or padding with silence.

1. **Prove the small pipeline first.** Make 20 clips, then 1–2 h of accepted data. Confirm that the chosen trainer reads stereo and aligned text correctly before scaling. Kyutai's official format uses **assistant left, user right**, with JSONL audio manifests and transcripts. This channel convention must be explicit in every artifact. [Training format](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/moshi-finetune).

2. **Write original scripts locally.** Use **Qwen3-8B through MLX-LM**, quantized for memory, or handwrite templates. The official 8B checkpoint declares **Apache 2.0**; run the script model and TTS sequentially to limit memory pressure. [Model](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B), [MLX-LM](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm). Generate everyday help, explanation, planning, correction and uncertainty scenarios. Require short spoken turns, no markdown/URLs/code, and an assistant reply policy per condition. Produce about **600 scenario families × 3 delivery variants × ~60 accepted seconds = ~30 conversation h**; adjust using measured duration, not word-count promises. Those figures are planning arithmetic, not a measured generation rate.

3. **Create counterfactual branches.** Within each family keep the critical **user words identical**, varying neutral/relaxed, rushed/energetic and tense/distressed delivery. Author different appropriate replies: casual concise phrasing, clear structured spoken steps, or calm validation plus one manageable next step. Add speed/volume-only controls and examples where the correct substantive answer stays the same. Separately create lexical formal/casual/slang variants and label them **lexical tests**, since the words differ. Do not train crude rules such as “anger means answer angrily,” “fast means anxious,” or “formal accent means professional.” Use human review to establish that the intended adaptation makes sense.

4. **Render isolated user events.** First choice: **Qwen3-TTS 1.7B CustomVoice through MLX-Audio**, using consistent voice identity and varied instruction prompts; audition compliance with emotion, pace and tone. Use multiple licensed presets/designed identities, crossed with every condition. **Fun-CosyVoice3 0.5B** is an alternate after validating the Metal port and voice-reference rights. Avoid unlicensed real-person clones; do not use ESD/IEMOCAP/Expresso samples as cloning prompts in a permissive release. Official capability tables distinguish Qwen's 1.7B instruction control from the smaller variants. [Qwen controls](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS), [MLX](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-audio), [CosyVoice model licence](https://huggingface.co/FunAudioLLM/Fun-CosyVoice3-0.5B-2512).

5. **Render assistant events separately.** Use the same renderer with a stable assistant identity, but appropriate **response** emotion/rate. Fix the assistant identity across branches so a classifier cannot solve the task by speaker identity. Vary user identity independently. Add short acknowledgments, natural repairs and occasional nonverbals only when appropriate. Do not rely on a single mixed Dia output as the ground-truth speaker channels. Dia is an optional naturalness reference; isolated per-event rendering is easier to audit. [Dia's transcript/nonverbal interface](https://github.com/nari-labs/dia).

6. **Assemble timing from events.** Store speaker, act, text, desired style, reference voice and a relative timing relation for every event. Example: “assistant acknowledgment begins 100 ms after the end of the user's word ‘tomorrow’”; “user correction starts during assistant phrase X; assistant yields after its next word.” Render first, **measure word timings**, then resolve these relations on a shared clock. Keep an initial majority of ordinary exchanges, with a planned minority of within-turn pauses, overlapping backchannels, interruptions and repairs; tune these proportions from licensed real examples rather than claiming guessed percentages are human statistics. Never allow accidental same-speaker collisions. Distinguish non-floor-taking backchannels from interruptions and side-talk. This follows a published measured-word-anchor design. [2026 synthesis harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28806v1).

7. **Align the actual waveform, not the intended script.** Run **Qwen3-ForcedAligner** on each isolated event, independently ASR it with **Qwen3-ASR or Parakeet**, reject omissions/additions, then shift all word times by the assembled event offset. Recompute labels after truncating interrupted turns; remove words not actually audible. Export matching-length stereo, the model's expected sample rate, and assistant-text timestamps; retain original event manifests and seeds for reproducibility. Let the Moshi preparation code handle tokenizer padding/delay conventions rather than inventing incompatible PAD placement. [Apple ASR/alignment tools](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-audio), [Moshi preparation](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/moshi-finetune).

8. **Quality-gate before counting hours.** Reject empty audio, clipping, wrong speaker, repeated text, hallucinated words, unstable voice and intended-style failures. Audit alignment around overlap boundaries by listening to both stems and the mix. Check distributions of measured rate, pitch, energy, pauses and overlap; volume-normalizing everything would erase a volume experiment. Have a human blind-rate a balanced sample for perceived delivery and reply appropriateness, including a sample of “accepted” clips. Record rejected/generated/accepted counts and active-speech durations. Keep all variants of one script family in the **same split**, and hold out voices, scenarios and at least one renderer for evaluation.

9. **Budget wall time honestly.** Define **RTF = generation seconds / active audio seconds**. At RTF 1, 30 h of rendered active speech takes about 30 generation h, before retries/alignment; RTF 2 takes about 60 h. Two padded channels are not necessarily 60 h of active speech. Measure a 10-minute balanced pilot on this M5 with models warm, plugged in, and no competing model loaded; extrapolate from **accepted output**, not advertised first-audio latency. MLX supports the components, but M5 sustained throughput remains unverified. [MLX implementation](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-audio).

**Licence risks for this recipe:** prefer owned scripts, permissive exact model checkpoints, and consented/designed voices. Archive licences and provenance. Do not mix in noncommercial emotional audio or seed voices and relabel the result Apache/MIT. Keep CANDOR-derived media/synthetic derivatives within its custom sharing restrictions. GPT/Azure-derived datasets need separate upstream review; they are unnecessary for this near-zero-cost local recipe. [CANDOR terms](https://huggingface.co/datasets/betterup/candor-raw), [Qwen model licence](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-CustomVoice), [Expresso terms](https://speechbot.github.io/expresso/).

## 2. Recommended evaluation set-up

**Existing regressions:** run **FDB v1.0 and v1.5** before/after fine-tuning, preserving identical stimuli, seeds, codec/rate and timing conventions. Report individual metrics rather than a single total. Use **ContextDialog** for conversational recall, **SD-Eval** for beyond-words reply relevance, and **EchoMind/StyleTalk** for delivery-dependent response quality where access terms permit. Use **LISTEN or VoxParadox** as acoustic-perception diagnostics; a spoken label-choice adapter is necessary if the model cannot accept the benchmark's normal instruction format. Add **EMO-Reasoning** when its release can be reproduced, with human checking of emotion scores. Run **FDB v2** once multi-turn live execution and examiner costs are acceptable. Run **v3 only with a tool-capable integration**. Sources and limitations are in F.

**Build new by extending the existing same-words harness:** add a **streaming Moshi/MLX replay adapter**, not a transcript-only proxy. Use at least **100 unseen script families**, each in three or more delivery conditions, including a smaller **consented human-recorded** subset and a renderer held out from training. Start each variant from the same context/state; stream the whole user waveform, including silence, while allowing the model to speak. Save both audio channels, generated text, sample/frame timestamps, runtime lag and seeds. Repeat paired cases across multiple seeds. Keep neutral controls and cases where no adaptation is needed.

Score four separate outcomes:

- **Words:** correct answer/task completion, appropriate specificity/structure, supportive phrasing, lexical register, unnecessary assumptions and overreaction. Blind human paired preference is the main small-budget measure; a local judge can triage, but must be calibrated against people. More lexical difference is not automatically better.
- **Delivery:** perceived calmness/support, naturalness, output speech rate, pauses, pitch/energy and whisper/volume behavior. When the user is distressed, reward a calm helpful response rather than matching their arousal. Preserve the source signal for volume tests; report microphone/gain controls.
- **Causal adaptation:** for each same-words family, compare whether the response changes in the **intended direction** while task correctness stays stable. Include voice-swapped and renderer-swapped controls, neutral/neutral repeats, and ASR-only/cascaded baselines. Report family-level paired differences with bootstrap confidence intervals; bootstrap families, not correlated variants.
- **Full-duplex behavior:** premature interruptions, response onset, yielding after interruption, noninterrupting backchannels, continuing after irrelevant side-talk, and silence failures. Score style changes both with and without overlaps so improved tone cannot conceal broken turn-taking.

Do **not** claim the old cascaded harness's behavior is proven for the new model until it captures audio while the model simultaneously emits output. If inference runs slower than realtime on the Mac, report **content-quality simulated-clock results separately from real wall-clock latency**. The essential new contribution is the combined streaming, paired-accommodation protocol; existing benchmarks already cover important parts of it.

## 3. The three biggest data risks

1. **Learning the wrong cue.** Synthetic speakers, renderer artifacts, explicit emotional words, age/accent stereotypes or scenario templates may reveal the label. The model can appear empathetic without hearing delivery. Cross voice with condition, hold text fixed for acoustic tests, hold out renderers/people/scenarios, and verify on real human pairs.
2. **Teaching bad conversational timing.** Alternating TTS plus arbitrary overlaps can teach premature interruption, excessive backchannels, or waiting through a real correction. Word alignment errors and channel bleed make this worse. Keep isolated stems, act/intent labels and measured timing; run duplex regression tests alongside style ratings.
3. **Unclear rights and provenance.** Downloadable audio, permissive code, generated voices and dataset headers confer different permissions. Noncommercial actor corpora, restricted seed voices, gated participant data and podcast reconstruction metadata can silently contaminate an intended reusable model/data release. Keep a per-source and per-reference rights manifest; the cleanest small pilot uses original scripts and consented or designed voices.
