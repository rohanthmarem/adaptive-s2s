Inside Moshi's ears

The same sentence, said three ways: excited, sad and plain. Moshi was made to stay silent and just listen, and every layer was recorded 12.5 times a second. This page walks from the machine, to its units, to what happens over time, to what it does with it.

1 · The machine

Sound goes in on the left. Each column is one layer.

Moshi never sees a waveform. The Mimi codec turns every 80 ms of audio into 8 small integers ("tokens"). Moshi looks each one up as a list of 4096 numbers and adds them up. That 4096-number list is passed through 32 layers; each layer reads it and adds its own edit. The last version goes to two heads: one picks the next word (or "stay silent"), the other picks Moshi's own audio tokens. Column colour = how well tone can be read from that layer after the user stops talking. Click a column.

2 · The units

4096 units at one layer, coloured by how they react to a sad voice versus an excited one

Each square is one of the 4096 numbers at this layer. Blue = it goes up when the voice is sad. Orange = it goes up when the voice is excited. Grey = it doesn't care. Strength is the effect size (Cohen's d): the gap between the two tones' averages divided by the normal take-to-take wobble. A d of 1 means the gap is as big as the wobble; 2 or more is a strong, reliable difference. The grid order is arbitrary, so look at how much lights up, not where.

3 · Watch it listen

One number per moment: is Moshi's state leaning sad or excited?

Squashing 4096 units into one meaningful number: first every unit is rescaled to the same spread (otherwise a few huge units drown out the rest), then we take the single direction in that 4096-D space that points from "excited voice" to "sad voice" (the average sad state minus the average excited state, learned from all 81 clips). At every 80 ms frame we measure how far the current state sits along that line. Above 0 = sad side, below 0 = excited side. The shaded band is when the voice is playing. Press play to hear each version with a moving playhead.

While the voice plays, the lines split almost at once and stay apart. After it stops (past the dashed lines) they drift back together on this axis, yet section 4's "after" view and the 84–88% probe show the tone is still there. The memory of how you sounded is kept along different directions from the live "hearing" signal, which is why this one line fades even though the information doesn't.

4 · The map

81 clips, with the words subtracted out

Every dot is one clip (9 sentences × 3 tones × 3 takes). For each sentence we subtracted its average state, which removes what the words contribute, then squashed 4096 dimensions down to the 2 that vary most (PCA). Nobody told the squashing about tone. If the colours still sort themselves into clumps, the voice is what moves Moshi most once the words are gone.

5 · A voice it was not trained on

The sad and excited directions were learned only from the Qwen "Ryan" voice. Do they still work on Grok's "Eve"?

These are the earlier xAI Grok TTS clips: a different engine, a different (female) voice, longer scripts, emotion requested with tags. Same projection as section 3, same directions, no refitting.

Every Grok clip, including three deliveries the axis was never shown (flat, hesitant, frustrated), placed by its average position while speaking:

Look at the excited ↔ sad axis around the middle layers. Excited lands at one end. Plain sits in the middle. Sad, flat and hesitant all land on the sad side, and flat and hesitant often go further than sad itself. So this axis is less "sadness" than energy: bright, fast and loud at one end; low, slow and quiet at the other. Psychologists call this arousal. It is the easiest emotional dimension to hear in a voice, and it is what Moshi tracks most clearly. Telling sad from flat or hesitant would need a second direction.

6 · The bottleneck

Moshi hears it, carries it, then barely uses it

How often a simple probe names the right tone (3 choices, so 33% is guessing), reading from each stage. Sentences are held out, so the probe can't cheat by recognising words.

The tone is clearly there in layer 31, the last layer before the heads. But read through the word head it mostly disappears, and Moshi's chance of starting to talk is the same for every tone (about 2.5% per frame), with "Yeah" as its top first word for all three. The information reaches the doorway and isn't used to choose what to say. That is the gap a fine-tune has to close: connect a signal that already exists, rather than teach Moshi to hear emotion.
